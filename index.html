Overview

People experiencing homelessness face a dramatically reduced life expectancy—often by decades—driven by extreme environmental exposure, fragmented healthcare access, and co-occurring chronic physical illness, serious mental illness, and substance use disorders. In Miami-Dade County, nearly 30% of households experience severe housing instability, and 67% of unhoused adults face active behavioral health or substance use conditions without stable access to primary care. To meet this need, the University of Miami Miller School of Medicine, Jackson Health System (Internal Medicine Urban Health Track and Combined Med-Peds Residencies), and Miami Street Medicine / Dade County Street Response created the Miami Street Medicine Training Initiative (MSMTI), supported by a Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA) grant. The multi-year track provides select residents with hands-on clinical training in the care of persons experiencing unsheltered homelessness (PEUH) directly where they live.

Curriculum

Community-partnered care is at the core of the training track, developing practical skills in trauma-informed, patient-centered medicine in non-traditional settings.

IM PGY-2 · Med-Peds PGY-3 · Elective Rotators

4-Week Clinical Immersion

Residents embed for a 4-week clinical immersion with the Miami Street Medicine and Dade County Street Response interprofessional team across outdoor encampments in Overtown, Downtown Miami, and Allapattah. Trainees deliver primary care, wound care, and bedside diagnostics (Butterfly iQ+ POCUS and i-STAT labs), while also seeing patients at the bi-weekly Doctors Within Borders Free Urgent Care Clinic in Liberty City and alongside the 24/7 Freedom House Mobile Crisis Team.

Jackson Memorial Hospital · Camillus Health Concern

Longitudinal Hospital-to-Street Continuity

Through our "Street Medicine on the Floors" consult service at Jackson Memorial Hospital, residents evaluate hospitalized unhoused patients at the bedside to prevent premature discharges and coordinate direct street follow-up. Residents also rotate at Camillus Health Concern (FQHC) and Camillus House to arrange medical respite beds, J-11 charity care coverage, and longitudinal panel tracking in REDCap.

IM PGY-3 · Med-Peds PGY-4 · IDEA Exchange

Street Psychiatry & Harm Reduction

During their senior immersion, residents join twice-weekly Community Psychiatry Rounds (led by Dr. Edward Suarez Jr. and Dr. Stephen McLeod-Bryant) providing field behavioral health care and curbside telepsychiatry. Residents also rotate at the IDEA Syringe Services Program (founded by Dr. Hansel Tookes) to deliver low-barrier sublingual and long-acting injectable buprenorphine, xylazine wound care, and mobile HIV/HCV treatment.

Didactics · POCUS · Linguistic Concordance

The "Street Classroom" Didactics & Simulation

Residents complete the Street Classroom curriculum covering field safety, verbal de-escalation, the HOUSED BEDS street history framework, extreme heat emergencies, and handheld ultrasound workshops. Training includes an experiential poverty simulation and medical Spanish and Haitian Creole communication workshops led by Dr. Victor Cueto.

LSGMI · UM Law Health Rights Clinic · FL DMV FLOW

Medical-Legal Partnership

In partnership with Legal Services of Greater Miami and the UM School of Law Health Rights Clinic, residents learn to address health-harming legal needs, draft medical affidavits for SSI/SSDI disability benefits, prevent unlawful evictions, and restore state IDs through the Florida DMV FLOW mobile unit.

Learning Objectives

Patient Care in Nontraditional Settings

  • Deliver trauma-informed primary and urgent care in street encampments and mobile clinics.
  • Perform bedside handheld ultrasound (POCUS) and field wound care.
  • Initiate low-barrier buprenorphine and adapt regimens to street living conditions.

Medical Knowledge

  • Manage tri-morbidity (co-occurring physical illness, mental illness, and substance use).
  • Treat acute street emergencies, including heat stroke, SSTIs, DVT, and overdose.
  • Assess decisional capacity and screen for HIV, HCV, and TB in field settings.

Interprofessional & Communication Skills

  • Collaborate within interprofessional teams alongside case managers and peer navigators.
  • Practice assertive street engagement and trauma-informed verbal de-escalation.
  • Provide culturally and linguistically concordant care in Spanish and Haitian Creole.

Professionalism

  • Uphold patient autonomy, dignity, and Miami Street Medicine's Anti-Exploitation Doctrine.
  • Mitigate bias and stigma toward unhoused patients and people who use drugs.
  • Maintain field safety protocols and clinician wellbeing practices.

Practice-Based Learning & Improvement

  • Track longitudinal patient panels and clinical outcomes in the REDCap registry.
  • Complete a mentored quality improvement or participatory research project.
  • Incorporate multi-source feedback from faculty and community partners.

Systems-Based Practice

  • Navigate J-11 charity care, FQHCs, medical respite, and Medicaid/SSI enrollment.
  • Partner with Medical-Legal Partnership attorneys and mobile ID services.
  • Advocate for structural policies supporting unhoused communities.

Research and Conferences

Track residents complete a mentored scholarly or quality improvement project through the Resident Scholarly Activity Program (RSAP) and the DCSR Participatory Research Institute, leveraging our longitudinal REDCap clinical registry. Residents are supported to present their work at regional and national conferences:

  • International Street Medicine Symposium (ISMS)

    Annual global symposium hosted by the Street Medicine Institute.

  • National Health Care for the Homeless Council

    National conference on HCH clinical practice, respite, and housing policy.

  • AMERSA Annual Conference

    Multidisciplinary addiction medicine and harm reduction research.

  • SGIM & AAMC

    Regional and national general internal medicine, Med-Peds, and GME meetings.

How to Apply

How do I apply to this training track?

Apply through ERAS to the UM / Jackson Health System Internal Medicine Residency or Combined Med-Peds Residency. All matched Med-Peds residents participate automatically; Categorical Internal Medicine applicants can express interest in the Urban Health / Street Medicine Track during interviews or intern orientation.

Is there a separate NRMP number in ERAS for the street medicine training track?

No. Rank the Categorical Internal Medicine code (1104140C0) or Categorical Med-Peds code (1104700C0).

University of Miami, Jackson Health System, and Miami Street Medicine Launch Street Medicine Residency Training Program

HRSA-25-078 Award 1 T35HP55565-01-00 $2.5M Five-Year Grant

MIAMI, FL — Building on more than five years of street-based outreach across Miami-Dade County, the University of Miami Miller School of Medicine, Jackson Health System, and Dade County Street Response / Miami Street Medicine have launched the Miami Street Medicine Training Initiative (MSMTI), supported by a five-year, $2.5 million HRSA grant (1 T35HP55565-01-00).

Led by Project Director Armen Henderson, MD, MBA and Co-Project Director Stefanie R. Brown, MD, MBA, together with Jonathan Tolentino, MD, MS-HPE, Kendra Van Kirk, MD, MAT, and Founding Director and Curriculum Co-Developer Dan Bergholz, MD, the track trains Internal Medicine and Med-Peds residents across street encampments, the Doctors Within Borders Urgent Care Clinic, Camillus Health Concern, the IDEA Syringe Services Program, and Jackson Memorial Hospital's inpatient street consult service.
