Miami Street Medicine Training Track

A longitudinal residency training track and community-partnered clinical immersion preparing primary care physicians to deliver consistent, trauma-informed care to people experiencing unsheltered homelessness across Miami-Dade County.

October 2025 · HRSA PCTE-RTSM Residency Training Initiative

Overview

People experiencing homelessness face a dramatically reduced life expectancy, often by two to three decades compared with housed peers. The causes are multifactorial and include unstable and extreme environmental living conditions, limited healthcare access, co-occurring mental health and substance use disorders, incarceration, linguistic barriers, and systemic inequities. Those without stable housing experience higher rates of uncontrolled cardiovascular and metabolic disease, infectious illnesses, severe skin and soft-tissue wounds, and heat-related injuries—making them one of the most medically vulnerable populations in the United States.

In Miami-Dade County—a diverse metropolitan region of 2.7 million residents (69.1% Hispanic/Latino and 16.9% Black/African American, with a substantial Haitian Creole-speaking community)—nearly 30% of households experience severe housing instability (compared with 19% across Florida and 18% nationally), and 66 of Florida's 67 counties are federally designated Primary Care Health Professional Shortage Areas. This crisis disproportionately affects individuals living with tri-morbidity: the simultaneous intersection of chronic physical disease, serious mental illness, and substance use disorder, with 67% reporting current and 86% reporting lifetime behavioral health or substance use conditions.

For these reasons, the University of Miami Miller School of Medicine and Jackson Health System / Jackson Memorial Hospital—across both the Internal Medicine Residency Program (Urban Health Track) and the Combined Internal Medicine-Pediatrics (Med-Peds) Residency Program—in partnership with Miami Street Medicine (MSM) and Dade County Street Response (DCSR), created the Miami Street Medicine Training Initiative (MSMTI), supported by a Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA) grant (HRSA-25-078, Award 1 T35HP55565-01-00). The MSMTI features a longitudinal multi-year street medicine training track (3 years for Categorical Internal Medicine Urban Health Track residents; 4 years for Categorical Med-Peds residents) who gain vital clinical, procedural, and advocacy skills in the care of persons experiencing homelessness (PEH) and, more specifically, persons experiencing unsheltered homelessness (PEUH).

Curriculum

The training track features community-partnered care at the core of the program—grounded in Radical Hospitality, Housing First, Harm Reduction, and Miami Street Medicine's Anti-Exploitation Doctrine—which ensures development of key skills in providing trauma-informed, patient-centered care in non-traditional settings.

IM Urban Health PGY-2 · Med-Peds PGY-3 · Elective Rotators

4-Week Clinical Immersion & Staggered Field Outreach

The primary field experience is a 4-week clinical immersion focused on Care for the Unsheltered Unhoused, where residents care for patients alongside the Dade County Street Response (DCSR) and Miami Street Medicine (MSM) clinical outreach teams based out of Liberty City/Allapattah (4300 NW 12th Ave, Miami, FL).

To prevent encampment overcrowding and honor patient privacy, resident field deployment follows a staggered 3-resident schedule embedded within a 4-person interprofessional street team. The team delivers consistent care directly in the street, under highway overpasses, at outdoor encampments across Overtown, Downtown Miami, and Lummus Park, and through mobile outreach units.

  • Weekly Street Rounds & Point-of-Care Diagnostics: Residents deliver essential primary care, chronic disease stabilization, field wound care and sharp debridement, heat illness triage, and bedside diagnostics using handheld Butterfly iQ+ ultrasound (POCUS) and Abbott i-STAT blood analyzers out of modular medical backpacks.
  • Doctors Within Borders (DWB) Free Urgent Care Clinic: Bi-weekly walk-in curbside urgent care clinic in Liberty City providing acute infection management, medication dispensing under Florida's Volunteer Health Care Provider Program (VHCPP sovereign immunity, F.S. § 766.1115), and warm handoffs to safety-net clinics.
  • Freedom House Mobile Crisis Team: 24/7 unarmed community crisis response answering behavioral health and acute social vulnerability calls across Miami-Dade County.
Jackson Memorial Hospital · Camillus Health Concern (FQHC)

Longitudinal Hospital-to-Street Continuity Clinic & Inpatient Consults

Throughout residency, track participants bridge acute hospitalization and street outreach through a bidirectional "Street Medicine on the Floors" model. At Jackson Memorial Hospital (JMH), residents work with hospitalist faculty on the Inpatient Street Medicine Consult Service, evaluating hospitalized unhoused patients at the bedside to treat acute withdrawal, prevent premature "against medical advice" (AMA) discharges, simplify medication regimens to once-daily heat-stable formulations, and schedule post-discharge street follow-up coordinates.

Residents also rotate at Camillus Health Concern (FQHC) and Camillus House (336 NW 5th Street, Miami, FL), coordinating post-hospitalization medical respite beds, Lazarus Project behavioral outreach, and J-11 Jackson Card charity-care financial enrollment while tracking their longitudinal patient panels in the MSM REDCap Clinical Registry and New Innovations.

IM Urban Health PGY-3 · Med-Peds PGY-4 · IDEA Exchange SSP

Street Psychiatry, Harm Reduction & Addiction Medicine

During their senior 4-week immersion block (Street Psychiatry, Harm Reduction, and Addressing Social & Legal Needs), track residents work with an interprofessional team of psychiatrists, psychologists, infectious disease and addiction medicine specialists, social workers, and peer navigators across street encampments and harm reduction sites:

  • Community Psychiatry Rounds & Curbside Telepsychiatry: Co-led twice weekly by Dr. Edward Suarez Jr., PsyD, LMHC, MBA and Dr. Stephen McLeod-Bryant, MD, residents perform field psychiatric evaluations at encampments, Camillus House, and the IDEA Clinic—practicing trauma-informed verbal de-escalation, street decisional capacity evaluation, long-acting injectable (LAI) antipsychotic maintenance, and real-time curbside telepsychiatry during Saturday street runs.
  • Harm Reduction & Addiction Medicine at the IDEA Exchange: Founded by Dr. Hansel Tookes, MD, MPH as Florida's first legal syringe services program (IDEA Exchange, 1668 NW 7th Ave, Miami, FL) and precepted by Dr. Tookes, Dr. Teresa Chueng, MD, MPH, and Dr. Katrina Ciraldo, MD, residents initiate low-barrier sublingual (8–16 mg) and long-acting injectable (Sublocade) buprenorphine, treat xylazine/fentanyl necrotizing wounds, distribute naloxone, and deliver Tele-Harm Reduction HIV/HCV care and PrEP.

The "Street Classroom" Didactic Curriculum, Simulation & Linguistic Concordance

All track residents complete the longitudinal Street Classroom curriculum, co-developed by DCSR Clinical Educator Dr. Dan Bergholz in collaboration with Dr. Armen Henderson, Dr. Stefanie Brown, Dr. Jonathan Tolentino, Dr. Kendra Van Kirk, and the UM/JMH faculty team. Synchronous case-based sessions convene on Wednesday evenings at 5:30 PM EST, complemented by hands-on procedural labs and simulations:

  • Core Didactic Trunk (T1–T7) & Specialty Branches (B01–B16): Covers Tri-Morbidity & Street Medicine Ethos, Assertive Engagement & Verbal De-Escalation, Field Safety & the 6-Level REDCap Flagging System, the HOUSED BEDS Street History & Adapted Physical Exam, Can't-Miss Street Conditions (Wound Care, SSTI, DVT, Extreme Heat), Rapid Buprenorphine Induction, Handheld POCUS Labs, and a 2-station OSCE checkout.
  • Poverty Simulation & Linguistic Concordance: Residents complete the Miami Coalition for the Homeless Community Action Poverty Simulation (CAPS) and interactive workshops led by Dr. Victor Cueto, MD, MS, MPH on cultural humility and medical Spanish and Haitian Creole communication.
View the HOUSED BEDS Mnemonic & 6-Level Street Triage Framework

Residents utilize seven standardized field pocket cards during street rounds and mobile urgent care shifts:

  • HOUSED BEDS Street History Framework: Housing & exact sleep coordinates; Outreach & case management connections; Unsheltered environmental exposures (heat, rain, vectors, footwear); Substance use, route, withdrawal risk & harm reduction supplies; Economics, income & benefits (SNAP, SSI/SSDI, J-11 card); Documents & government ID status (FL DMV FLOW eligibility); Behavioral health, trauma & psychiatric history; Eating, food/water security & medication storage reality; Disability, mobility, vision/hearing & Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) screen; Safety, violence, intimate partner violence (IPV) & legal barriers.
  • 6-Level Street Clinical Triage & REDCap Flagging System: Standardizes field acuity from Level 1 (Immediate 911 / Life-Threatening Emergency) and Level 2 (Urgent Mobile Crisis / Same-Day ED Transfer) through Levels 3–6 (Subacute Curbside Stabilization, Routine Street Primary Care, Case Management Priority, and Peer Check-In), paired with dynamic safety flagging in REDCap.
LSGMI · UM Law Health Rights Clinic · FL DMV FLOW

Medical-Legal Partnership (MLP)

Track residents receive formal training in Medical-Legal Partnership collaboration alongside public interest attorneys and DCSR case managers through partnerships with Legal Services of Greater Miami, Inc. (LSGMI), the University of Miami School of Law Health Rights Clinic, and the UM Miller School of Medicine Human Rights Clinic. Residents learn to screen for health-harming legal needs on the street, prepare medical-legal documentation for SSI/SSDI disability applications and appeals, support unlawful eviction defense and Fair Housing accommodations, and assist patients in recovering birth certificates and official state identification cards through the Florida DMV FLOW (Florida Licensing on Wheels) mobile unit.

Learning Objectives

Aligned with ACGME Internal Medicine and Combined Internal Medicine-Pediatrics Milestones and HRSA PCTE-RTSM program objectives, residents develop competency across six core domains:

Patient Care in Nontraditional Settings

  • Deliver trauma-informed, patient-centered primary and urgent care directly on sidewalks, under highway overpasses, in encampments, and inside mobile medical units.
  • Conduct a focused street history and physical exam using the HOUSED BEDS framework and classify acuity using the 6-Level Street Triage System.
  • Perform bedside handheld Point-of-Care Ultrasound (POCUS) to differentiate soft-tissue abscess from cellulitis/phlegmon and perform 2-point DVT compression.
  • Initiate low-barrier sublingual and long-acting injectable buprenorphine and manage severe injection-related and xylazine-associated wounds in field settings.
  • Adapt chronic disease regimens to street realities, selecting heat-stable, once-daily medications and mitigating hypoglycemia or dehydration risks in extreme heat.

Medical Knowledge

  • Explain the epidemiology, pathophysiology, and mortality drivers of tri-morbidity (co-occurring chronic physical illness, serious mental illness, and substance use disorder).
  • Recognize and manage "can't-miss" street emergencies, including heat stroke, necrotizing soft-tissue infections, acute opioid/sedative overdose, and alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal.
  • Evaluate decisional capacity on the street, distinguishing eccentric survival behaviors from acute life-threatening psychosis or medical delirium.
  • Apply evidence-based screening and treatment protocols for HIV, hepatitis B/C, tuberculosis, syphilis, and ectoparasitic infestations in unsheltered populations.

Interprofessional and Communication Skills

  • Collaborate effectively within the interprofessional street team alongside lived-experience Peer Navigators, Case Managers, street psychiatrists, nurses, and students.
  • Demonstrate assertive yet non-coercive street engagement, motivational interviewing, and trauma-informed verbal de-escalation during high-stress encounters.
  • Deliver linguistically and culturally concordant care for Spanish- and Haitian Creole-speaking patients.
  • Execute closed-loop warm handoffs between inpatient hospital teams, emergency departments, medical respite facilities, and street outreach teams.

Professionalism

  • Uphold Miami Street Medicine's Anti-Exploitation Doctrine and Radical Hospitality, honoring each unsheltered person's autonomy, privacy, and right to decline care.
  • Recognize and mitigate implicit and explicit bias, diagnostic overshadowing, and stigmatizing language toward individuals who use drugs or live with severe mental illness.
  • Apply sustainable field safety practices, dynamic REDCap safety flagging, and structured debriefing to prevent moral injury and clinician burnout.

Practice-Based Learning and Improvement

  • Utilize the MSM REDCap Clinical Registry to track longitudinal patient panels, monitor blood pressure and HbA1c trajectories, and close post-discharge follow-up gaps.
  • Design and execute a mentored Quality Improvement (QI) or Community-Based Participatory Research (CBPR) project through the Resident Scholarly Activity Program (RSAP).
  • Incorporate multi-source feedback from faculty preceptors, case managers, and community observers to continuously refine clinical practice.

Systems-Based Practice

  • Navigate local, state, and federal safety-net systems, including the J-11 Jackson Card charity care pathway, Camillus Health Concern (FQHC), and Medicaid/SSI/SSDI enrollment.
  • Partner with Medical-Legal Partnership (MLP) attorneys and the FL DMV FLOW mobile unit to resolve health-harming legal needs and restore government identification.
  • Understand the statutory framework of Florida's Volunteer Health Care Provider Program (VHCPP sovereign immunity, F.S. § 766.1115) and the Infectious Disease Elimination Act.

Research and Conferences

Track residents are expected to complete a mentored scholarly project—such as a Community-Based Participatory Research (CBPR) study, clinical outcomes evaluation, or quality improvement initiative—through the 8-session Resident Scholarly Activity Program (RSAP) co-led by Dr. Jonathan Tolentino and Dr. Victor Cueto, in partnership with the Dade County Street Response Participatory Research Institute and the IDEA Lab. Residents have mentored access to the IRB-approved Miami Street Medicine REDCap Clinical Informatics Registry (437+ longitudinal cohort patients and 4,000+ street encounters).

How to Apply

How do I apply to this training track?

Prospective residency applicants apply through the Electronic Residency Application Service (ERAS) to either the University of Miami / Jackson Health System Internal Medicine Residency Program or the University of Miami / Jackson Health System Combined Internal Medicine-Pediatrics (Med-Peds) Residency Program.

  • Combined Med-Peds Applicants: All categorical Med-Peds residents matching into the UM/Jackson Med-Peds program (5 residents per year) participate in the longitudinal 4-year Miami Street Medicine Training Initiative.
  • Categorical Internal Medicine Applicants (Urban Health Track): Applicants interested in the 3-year Street Medicine / Urban Health Track (4 residents per class year) should apply to the Categorical Internal Medicine program in ERAS and express interest in the Urban Health Track / Miami Street Medicine Training Initiative during the interview season or during intern orientation after matching.

Is there a separate NRMP number in ERAS for the street medicine training track?

No. There is no separate NRMP number in ERAS exclusively for the street medicine training track. Applicants rank the official Categorical Internal Medicine NRMP code (1104140C0) or Categorical Internal Medicine-Pediatrics NRMP code (1104700C0).

Can categorical residents outside the track participate in Street Medicine?

Yes. Categorical Internal Medicine, Emergency Medicine, Family Medicine, and Psychiatry residents at UM/Jackson Memorial Hospital may request the 2- to 4-week Street Medicine Elective Rotation through their Program Coordinator and MSMTI Program Manager Dr. Oseiga Odafen. Fourth-year medical students may also enroll in the 4-week Street Classroom Senior Elective.

University of Miami, Jackson Health System, and Miami Street Medicine Launch HRSA-Funded Street Medicine Residency Training Initiative

HRSA-25-078 Award 1 T35HP55565-01-00 $2,499,995 Five-Year Grant

MIAMI, FL — Building on more than five years of street-based medical outreach, mobile crisis response, and harm reduction across Miami-Dade County, the University of Miami Miller School of Medicine, Jackson Health System / Jackson Memorial Hospital, and Dade County Street Response (DCSR) / Miami Street Medicine (MSM) have launched the Miami Street Medicine Training Initiative (MSMTI), supported by a five-year, $2.5 million grant (1 T35HP55565-01-00) from the Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA) Bureau of Health Workforce under the Primary Care Training and Enhancement: Residency Training in Street Medicine (PCTE-RTSM, HRSA-25-078) program.

Led by Principal Investigator and Project Director Armen Henderson, MD, MBA (Assistant Professor of Clinical Medicine and Founder/CEO of Dade County Street Response) and Co-Project Director Stefanie R. Brown, MD, MBA (Internal Medicine Residency Program Director and Section Chief of Med-Peds), in partnership with Med-Peds Program Director Jonathan Tolentino, MD, MS-HPE, Urban Health Track Director Kendra Van Kirk, MD, MAT, and Miami Street Medicine Founding Director and Curriculum Co-Developer Dan Bergholz, MD, the initiative embeds formal street medicine training across both the Internal Medicine (Urban Health Track) and Combined Internal Medicine-Pediatrics residency programs.

Addressing South Florida's Unsheltered Health Crisis

In Miami-Dade County, nearly 30% of households face severe housing instability, and unsheltered individuals confront extreme tropical heat, high rates of untreated hypertension (72% baseline prevalence in MSM's street cohort), and a 67% current / 86% lifetime prevalence of mental health and substance use disorders. Since 2020, Miami Street Medicine and Dade County Street Response have cared for more than 1,300 unique unsheltered patients across 4,000+ street encounters—demonstrating a statistically significant -9.25 mmHg reduction in systolic blood pressure (p < 0.01) and 45.3% full hypertension control delivered directly on the street.

A Seamless Hospital-to-Street Educational Ecosystem

Through two 4-week community clinical immersions, longitudinal continuity panels, and the 23-module "Street Classroom" curriculum, residents train across four integrated clinical environments:

  • Dade County Street Response & Miami Street Medicine: Weekly street rounds across Overtown, Downtown Miami, and countywide encampments, the Doctors Within Borders Free Urgent Care Clinic in Liberty City, and the 24/7 Freedom House Mobile Crisis Team.
  • Camillus Health Concern (FQHC) & Camillus House: Comprehensive Health Care for the Homeless primary care, Lazarus Project behavioral outreach, and post-hospitalization medical respite beds.
  • IDEA Syringe Services Program (IDEA Exchange): Florida's first legal syringe exchange, founded by Dr. Hansel Tookes, training residents in low-barrier sublingual and long-acting injectable buprenorphine, xylazine/fentanyl wound care, and Tele-Harm Reduction HIV/HCV care.
  • Jackson Memorial Hospital Inpatient Street Medicine Consult Service: "Street Medicine on the Floors" bedside consults preventing premature discharges and linking hospitalized unhoused patients to J-11 Jackson Card coverage and street follow-up.
