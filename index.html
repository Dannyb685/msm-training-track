Overview

HRSA-25-078 • Award 1 T35HP55565-01-00

People experiencing homelessness face a dramatically reduced life expectancy—often by two to three decades compared with housed peers—with mortality rates up to nine times higher among young adults and 3.5 times higher across all age groups. The causes are multifactorial and rooted in structural inequities: unstable and extreme environmental living conditions, fragmented healthcare access, co-occurring serious mental illness and substance use disorders, criminalization and incarceration, linguistic barriers, and pervasive institutional mistrust.

Those without stable housing experience disproportionately high burdens of uncontrolled cardiovascular and metabolic disease, severe skin and soft-tissue infections, communicable diseases (HIV, hepatitis C, tuberculosis), heat-related illness, and traumatic injury—frequently compounded by tri-morbidity (the simultaneous intersection of chronic physical illness, severe mental illness, and substance use disorder).

In Miami-Dade County—a diverse metropolitan region of 2.7 million residents (69.1% Hispanic/Latino and 16.9% Black/African American, with a substantial Haitian Creole-speaking community)—nearly 30% of households experience severe housing instability (compared with 19% across Florida and 18% nationally), 13.4% of residents under age 65 lack health insurance, and 66 of Florida's 67 counties are federally designated Primary Care Health Professional Shortage Areas (HPSAs). Among people experiencing homelessness in Miami-Dade, 67% report current and 86% report lifetime mental health or substance use disorders, yet traditional clinic walls remain inaccessible due to transportation barriers, rigid appointment systems, lack of government identification, and prior stigmatizing healthcare encounters.

For these reasons, the University of Miami Miller School of Medicine and Jackson Health System / Jackson Memorial Hospital—across both the Internal Medicine Residency Program (Urban Health Track) and the Combined Internal Medicine-Pediatrics (Med-Peds) Residency Program—in partnership with Miami Street Medicine (MSM) and Dade County Street Response (DCSR), created the Miami Street Medicine Training Initiative (MSMTI), supported by a five-year, $2.5 million Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA) Primary Care Training and Enhancement: Residency Training in Street Medicine (PCTE-RTSM) grant (HRSA-25-078, Award 1 T35HP55565-01-00).

The MSMTI features a longitudinal multi-year street medicine training track (3 years for Categorical Internal Medicine Urban Health Track residents; 4 years for Categorical Med-Peds residents) alongside structured 2- to 4-week elective blocks for broader Internal Medicine and Emergency Medicine residents. Trainees gain vital clinical, procedural, behavioral, and structural advocacy skills in the direct care of persons experiencing homelessness (PEH) and, specifically, persons experiencing unsheltered homelessness (PEUH) directly where they live—under highway overpasses, in outdoor encampments, at drop-in syringe services, and in mobile urgent care units.

Foundational Ethos: Radical Hospitality, Reality-Based Medicine & The Anti-Exploitation Doctrine

Guided by the Street Medicine Institute's philosophy to "Go to the People", MSMTI trains residents in Radical Hospitality, Housing First, Harm Reduction, and Reality-Based Clinical Decision-Making (adapting diagnostic and therapeutic plans to each patient's actual street ecology, heat exposure, and storage constraints). Every field encounter is governed by Miami Street Medicine's Anti-Exploitation Doctrine: unsheltered patients are our neighbors, not clinical teaching material. Patient dignity, privacy, and voluntary consent unconditionally supersede educational objectives.

Curriculum

Community-Partnered Care & Street Classroom

The Miami Street Medicine Training Track places community-partnered, interprofessional care at the core of residency education. Rather than treating street medicine as a purely observational experience, the curriculum scaffolds trainees from foundational safety, de-escalation, and simulation bootcamps in PGY-1 to intensive 4-week community-based clinical immersions in PGY-2, PGY-3, and PGY-4, supported throughout residency by the "Street Classroom" didactic trunk and hospital-to-street longitudinal continuity panels.

4-Week Clinical Immersion & Staggered Field Outreach (Rotation 1: Care for the Unsheltered Unhoused)

IM UHT PGY-2 • Med-Peds PGY-3 • Elective Rotators

The foundational field experience is a dedicated 4-week clinical immersion focused on Care for the Unsheltered Unhoused (completed in PGY-2 for Urban Health Track Internal Medicine residents, PGY-3 for Combined Med-Peds residents, and as a 2- to 4-week block for elective rotators). Residents embed directly with the Dade County Street Response (DCSR) and Miami Street Medicine (MSM) clinical outreach teams headquartered in Liberty City/Allapattah (4300 NW 12th Ave, Miami, FL).

To prevent encampment overcrowding and protect psychological safety on the street, resident field deployment is governed by a strict 3-Resident Staggered Field Schedule embedded within a tight 4-Person Interprofessional Street Team (1 Attending Physician, 1 Resident Physician in a Clinical Support Role, 1 Professional Case Manager, and 1 salaried Lived-Experience Peer Navigator, accompanied by Spanish and Haitian Creole Language Observers).

  • Weekly Street Rounds (Overtown, Downtown Miami, Lummus Park & Camillus Perimeters): Residents deliver direct curbside primary care, hypertension and diabetes stabilization (avoiding hypoglycemia/lactic acidosis pitfalls in food-insecure patients), field wound care and sharp debridement, heat illness triage, and point-of-care diagnostics using handheld Butterfly iQ+ ultrasound (POCUS) and Abbott i-STAT blood analyzers out of modular medical backpacks.
  • Doctors Within Borders (DWB) Free Urgent Care Clinic (Liberty City): Bi-weekly walk-in curbside urgent care clinic operating in an underserved Black and Afro-Caribbean neighborhood, providing acute infection management, point-of-care testing, medication dispensing under Florida's sovereign immunity Volunteer Health Care Provider Program (VHCPP, F.S. § 766.1115), and warm handoffs to safety-net primary care.
  • Freedom House Mobile Crisis Team (24/7 Unarmed Crisis Response): Residents rotate alongside DCSR's unarmed community crisis responders answering mental health, substance use, and extreme social vulnerability calls across Miami-Dade County as a compassionate alternative to law enforcement response.

Longitudinal Hospital-to-Street Continuity Clinic & Inpatient Consult Service

Jackson Memorial Hospital • Camillus Health Concern (FQHC)

Unsheltered patients experience high rates of emergency department recidivism, premature "against medical advice" (AMA) discharges, and post-hospitalization mortality when discharged back to the pavement with prescriptions they cannot fill or wound care instructions requiring running water. MSMTI trains residents to close this gap through a bidirectional "Street Medicine on the Floors" continuity model:

  • Jackson Memorial Hospital (JMH) Inpatient Street Medicine Consult Service: Led by hospitalist faculty Dr. Armen Henderson and Dr. Joshua Laban, residents evaluate hospitalized unsheltered patients at the bedside, address untreated withdrawal or behavioral distress driving premature discharges, simplify discharge regimens to once-daily heat-stable formulations, and schedule concrete post-discharge street follow-up coordinates.
  • Camillus Health Concern (FQHC) & Camillus House Medical Respite: Residents rotate at Camillus Health Concern (336 NW 5th Street, Miami, FL), a federally qualified Health Care for the Homeless clinic and Lazarus Project behavioral health partner, coordinating post-hospitalization medical respite beds, emergency shelter intake, and transitional housing transitions.
  • "J-11 Jackson Card" Charity Care & REDCap Longitudinal Panel Tracking: Working alongside DCSR Case Management Lead Fallon L. Green and Clinic Manager Hana Abdulla, MSW, residents navigate the Jackson Health System financial assistance pathway ("J-11 Jackson Card") to unlock outpatient specialty clinics, imaging, and pharmacy access, while tracking their assigned longitudinal patient panel in the MSM REDCap Clinical Registry and New Innovations.

Street Psychiatry, Harm Reduction & Addiction Medicine (Rotation 2)

IM UHT PGY-3 • Med-Peds PGY-4 • IDEA Exchange SSP

In their senior immersion block (PGY-3 for Urban Health Track IM residents; PGY-4 for Med-Peds residents), trainees complete a dedicated 4-week rotation focused on Street Psychiatry, Harm Reduction, and Addressing Social & Legal Needs, integrating field behavioral health with evidence-based addiction medicine.

  • Community Psychiatry Rounds & Curbside Telepsychiatry: Co-led twice weekly by Dr. Edward Suarez Jr., PsyD, LMHC, MBA (UM Department of Psychiatry) and Dr. Stephen McLeod-Bryant, MD (DCSR Director of Mental Health Services), residents conduct field psychiatric evaluations at street encampments, Camillus House, and the IDEA Clinic. Trainees master trauma-informed verbal de-escalation, street decisional capacity assessments, long-acting injectable (LAI) antipsychotic maintenance, and real-time curbside telepsychiatry consultations during Saturday street runs.
  • IDEA Syringe Services Program (IDEA Exchange) & Mobile Harm Reduction: Founded by Dr. Hansel Tookes, MD, MPH as Florida's first legal syringe exchange (1668 NW 7th Ave, Miami, FL) and precepted by Dr. Tookes, Dr. Teresa Chueng, MD, MPH, and Dr. Katrina Ciraldo, MD, residents rotate on the IDEA fixed clinic, mobile syringe exchange unit, and wound care clinic. Trainees initiate low-barrier sublingual (8–16 mg) and long-acting injectable (Sublocade) buprenorphine, distribute intranasal naloxone and fentanyl/xylazine test strips, manage xylazine-associated necrotizing skin and soft-tissue infections, and deliver Tele-Harm Reduction HIV/HCV treatment and PrEP.

The "Street Classroom" Didactic Trunk, Simulation & Linguistic Concordance

7 Trunk Modules • 16 Branches • 51 Vignettes • POCUS Lab

All track residents and elective rotators complete the Street Classroom curriculum, co-developed by DCSR Clinical Educator Dr. Dan Bergholz in collaboration with Dr. Armen Henderson, Dr. Stefanie Brown, Dr. Jonathan Tolentino, Dr. Kendra Van Kirk, and the UM/JMH specialty faculty. Synchronous didactic and case-based sessions convene on Wednesday evenings at 5:30 PM EST (engineered with a 30-minute QGenda travel buffer following 5:00 PM inpatient sign-out at Jackson Memorial Hospital and the Miami VA), complemented by asynchronous Canvas/Blackboard LMS self-study modules and hands-on workshops:

  • 7-Session Core Didactic Trunk (T1–T7): Covers (1) Orientation, Tri-Morbidity & Street Medicine Ethos; (2) Assertive Engagement & Trauma-Informed Verbal De-Escalation; (3) Field Safety & the 6-Level REDCap Flagging System; (4) The HOUSED BEDS Street History Mnemonic & Adapted Street Physical Exam; (5) Can't-Miss Street Conditions (Wound Care, SSTI, DVT, Extreme Heat); (6) Addiction Essentials & Rapid Buprenorphine Induction; and (7) Clinical 2-Station OSCE Checkout & Resident Burnout Prevention.
  • 16 Specialty Branch Modules (B01–B16) & 51-Case Clinical Vignette Bank: Deep-dive modules spanning Handheld POCUS Lab (Abscess vs. Phlegmon & 2-Point DVT Compression), Advanced Care Coordination, Street Psychiatry & SMI Triage, Harm Reduction & LAI Buprenorphine, Intimate Partner Violence (IPV) & Human Trafficking Field Screening, Inpatient-to-Street Transitions, Heat Emergencies & Provider Needle-Stick Safety, and Foot Care / Environmental Exposure.
  • Experiential Poverty Simulation & Linguistic Concordance: In PGY-1, residents participate in the Miami Coalition for the Homeless Community Action Poverty Simulation (CAPS) and interactive workshops led by Dr. Victor Cueto, MD, MS, MPH on cultural humility and medical Spanish and Haitian Creole communication, paired with longitudinal field feedback from community language observers.
Explore the HOUSED BEDS Mnemonic & 6-Level Street Triage / REDCap Flagging System

Every trainee carries 7 Waterproof Field Pocket Cards standardizing clinical assessment and team safety across all outreach sites:

  • HOUSED BEDS Street History Framework: Housing & exact sleep location/coordinates; Outreach & case management connections; Unsheltered environmental exposures (extreme heat, rain, vector exposure, shoes/socks); Substance use, route, withdrawal risk & harm reduction supplies; Economics, income & benefits (SNAP, SSI/SSDI, J-11 card); Documents & government ID status (FL DMV FLOW eligibility); Behavioral health, trauma & psychiatric history; Eating, food/water security & medication storage reality; Disability, mobility, vision/hearing & Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) screen; Safety, violence, intimate partner violence (IPV) & legal barriers.
  • 6-Level Street Clinical Triage & REDCap Behavioral Flagging System: Standardizes medical acuity from Level 1 (Immediate 911 / Life-Threatening Emergency) and Level 2 (Urgent Mobile Crisis / Same-Day ED Transfer) through Levels 3–6 (Subacute Curbside Stabilization, Routine Street Primary Care, Case Management Priority, and Peer Check-In), paired with a dynamic 6-tier behavioral safety flag in REDCap that protects outreach teams while explicitly preventing permanent punitive patient discharges.

Medical-Legal Partnership (MLP) & Structural Advocacy

LSGMI • UM Law Health Rights Clinic • Human Rights Clinic

Unresolved civil and administrative legal needs—such as wrongful benefit terminations, lack of vital identification documents, unlawful evictions, municipal "camping ban" citations, and immigration barriers—directly undermine medical stabilization. Track residents receive formal didactic and experiential training in Medical-Legal Partnership (MLP) collaboration alongside public interest attorneys, law students, and DCSR case managers:

  • Legal Services of Greater Miami, Inc. (LSGMI) & UM School of Law Health Rights Clinic: Residents learn to screen for health-harming legal needs on the street and draft rigorous medical-legal supporting documentation for SSI/SSDI disability applications and appeals, Medicaid/Medicare coverage disputes, unlawful eviction defense, and reasonable housing accommodations under the Fair Housing Act.
  • Vital Document Recovery & FL DMV FLOW Mobile Units: Trainees work with DCSR case managers to help unsheltered patients replace lost birth certificates and obtain official Florida state identification cards through the Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles Florida Licensing on Wheels (FLOW) mobile unit—a prerequisite for shelter entry, J-11 Jackson Card enrollment, and permanent supportive housing.
  • UM Miller School of Medicine Human Rights Clinic & Policy Advocacy: Residents have opportunities to train in forensic medical and psychological evaluations for asylum seekers and survivors of trafficking, and to participate in municipal and state health policy advocacy addressing extreme heat protections, decriminalization of homelessness, and harm reduction access.

Learning Objectives

ACGME Core Competencies • Constructive Alignment

Modeled on ACGME Internal Medicine and Combined Internal Medicine-Pediatrics Milestones and mapped directly to HRSA Notice of Funding Opportunity HRSA-25-078 Objectives 1.1 through 3.4, track residents achieve competency across six core domains evaluated via direct bedside observation, New Innovations rotation evaluations, Spanish/Haitian Creole linguistic concordance rubrics, and a capstone 2-station Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE):

Patient Care in Nontraditional Settings

  • Deliver trauma-informed, patient-centered primary and urgent care directly on sidewalks, under highway overpasses, in encampments, and inside mobile medical units.
  • Conduct a focused street history and physical exam using the HOUSED BEDS framework and classify acuity using the 6-Level Street Triage System.
  • Perform bedside handheld Point-of-Care Ultrasound (POCUS) to differentiate soft-tissue abscess from cellulitis/phlegmon and perform 2-point lower-extremity DVT compression.
  • Initiate low-barrier sublingual and long-acting injectable (LAI) buprenorphine and manage severe injection-related and xylazine-associated wounds in field settings.
  • Adapt chronic disease regimens to street realities (e.g., selecting heat-stable, once-daily antihypertensives and avoiding hypoglycemia or dehydration risk in extreme tropical heat).

Medical Knowledge

  • Explain the epidemiology, pathophysiology, and mortality drivers of tri-morbidity (co-occurring chronic physical illness, serious mental illness, and substance use disorder) among unsheltered populations.
  • Recognize and manage "can't-miss" street emergencies, including exertional and non-exertional heat stroke, necrotizing soft-tissue infections, acute opioid/sedative overdose, alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal, and frostbite/immersion foot.
  • Evaluate decisional capacity on the street, distinguishing eccentric survival behaviors from acute life-threatening psychosis or medical delirium.
  • Apply evidence-based infectious disease screening and treatment protocols for HIV, hepatitis B/C, latent and active tuberculosis, syphilis, and ectoparasitic infestations in congregate and unsheltered settings.

Interprofessional and Communication Skills

  • Practice effectively within the 4-person interprofessional street team alongside lived-experience Peer Navigators, Case Managers, street psychiatrists, nurses, and medical students.
  • Demonstrate assertive yet non-coercive street engagement, motivational interviewing, and trauma-informed verbal de-escalation during high-stress behavioral encounters.
  • Deliver linguistically and culturally concordant care for Spanish- and Haitian Creole-speaking patients, incorporating structured feedback from community language observers.
  • Execute closed-loop warm handoffs between inpatient hospital teams, emergency departments, medical respite facilities, and street outreach teams.

Professionalism

  • Uphold Miami Street Medicine's Anti-Exploitation Doctrine and Radical Hospitality, honoring each unsheltered person's autonomy, territory, and right to decline care or learner participation without penalty.
  • Recognize and mitigate implicit and explicit bias, diagnostic overshadowing, and stigmatizing language toward individuals who use drugs, live with severe mental illness, or experience criminal-legal involvement.
  • Apply sustainable personal safety practices, dynamic REDCap safety flagging, and structured peer debriefing to prevent moral injury, compassion fatigue, and clinician burnout.

Practice-Based Learning and Improvement

  • Utilize the MSM REDCap Clinical Registry to track longitudinal patient panels, monitor blood pressure and HbA1c trajectories, and close post-hospitalization follow-up gaps.
  • Design and execute a mentored Quality Improvement (QI) or Community-Based Participatory Research (CBPR) project through the 8-session Resident Scholarly Activity Program (RSAP).
  • Synthesize feedback from multi-source 360-degree evaluations (attending faculty, case managers, peer navigators, and language observers) to refine field practice.

Systems-Based Practice

  • Navigate local, state, and federal safety-net systems, including the J-11 Jackson Card charity care pathway, Camillus Health Concern (FQHC), Miami-Dade Homeless Trust Continuum of Care, and Medicaid/SSI/SSDI enrollment.
  • Collaborate with Medical-Legal Partnership (MLP) attorneys and the FL DMV FLOW mobile unit to resolve health-harming legal needs and restore government identification documents.
  • Understand the statutory framework of Florida's Volunteer Health Care Provider Program (VHCPP sovereign immunity, F.S. § 766.1115) and the Infectious Disease Elimination Act (IDEA).

Research and Conferences

RSAP • DCSR Participatory Research Institute • REDCap Registry

Scholarly inquiry in the Miami Street Medicine Training Track is anchored in Community-Based Participatory Research (CBPR) and health equity quality improvement. Every track resident participates in the structured 8-session Resident Scholarly Activity Program (RSAP) co-led by Dr. Jonathan Tolentino and Dr. Victor Cueto, partnered with the Dade County Street Response Participatory Research Institute and the IDEA Lab.

Residents have mentored access to the IRB-approved Miami Street Medicine REDCap Clinical Informatics Registry (encompassing longitudinal demographic, social determinant, diagnostic, pharmacologic, and clinical outcome data across 437+ longitudinal cohort patients and 4,000+ street encounters). In keeping with our Anti-Exploitation Doctrine, all research questions are co-designed with community advisory input to ensure findings directly improve clinical delivery, resource allocation, and public policy for unhoused people in South Florida.

  • Knowledge, Attitudes, and Beliefs Regarding Medication-Assisted Treatment Among People Experiencing Unsheltered Homelessness in Miami, Florida

    Aging Medicine (2024) • Characterized accelerated geriatric syndromes, cardiovascular disease burden, and neurocognitive vulnerability among older unsheltered adults in South Florida.

  • Tele-Harm Reduction for Rapid Initiation of Antiretrovirals and Medications for Opioid Use Disorder Among People Who Inject Drugs

    Annals of Medicine / NIDA Avenir Research Program (Dr. Hansel Tookes, Dr. Edward Suarez Jr., Dr. Teresa Chueng et al.) • Pioneered syringe-exchange-embedded telehealth and peer navigation to achieve rapid viral suppression and MOUD retention.

How to Apply

ERAS & NRMP Match Guidance

How do I apply to this training track?

Prospective residency applicants apply through the Electronic Residency Application Service (ERAS) to either the University of Miami / Jackson Health System Internal Medicine Residency Program or the University of Miami / Jackson Health System Combined Internal Medicine-Pediatrics (Med-Peds) Residency Program.

  • Combined Med-Peds Applicants: All categorical Med-Peds residents matching into the UM/Jackson Med-Peds program (5 residents per year) are automatically enrolled in the longitudinal 4-year Miami Street Medicine Training Initiative (MSMTI).
  • Categorical Internal Medicine Applicants (Urban Health Track): Applicants interested in the 3-year Street Medicine / Urban Health Track (4 residents per class year) should apply to the Categorical Internal Medicine program in ERAS and indicate their interest in the Urban Health Track / Miami Street Medicine Training Initiative during the interview season or upon matching during intern orientation.

Is there a separate NRMP number in ERAS for the street medicine training track?

No. Just like the Yale Street Medicine Training Track, there is no separate NRMP match code exclusively for the street medicine track. Applicants rank the official Categorical Internal Medicine NRMP code (1104140C0) or Categorical Internal Medicine-Pediatrics NRMP code (1104700C0). Selection into the Internal Medicine Urban Health / Street Medicine cohort is finalized with matched residents prior to the start of intern year, while all matched Med-Peds residents participate longitudinally.

Can categorical residents outside the track or visiting medical students rotate with Miami Street Medicine?

Yes. Categorical Internal Medicine, Emergency Medicine, Family Medicine, and Psychiatry residents at UM/Jackson Memorial Hospital may request the 2- to 4-week Street Medicine Elective Rotation through their Program Coordinator and MSMTI Program Manager Dr. Oseiga Odafen (capped at 3 resident rotators per 4-week block to preserve encampment safety and patient dignity). Fourth-year medical students may also enroll in the 4-week Street Classroom Senior Elective through the University of Miami Miller School of Medicine Office of Student Affairs.

University of Miami, Jackson Health System, and Miami Street Medicine Launch HRSA-Funded Street Medicine Residency Training Initiative

HRSA-25-078 Award 1 T35HP55565-01-00 $2,499,995 Five-Year Grant Project Period: 2025–2030

MIAMI, FL — Building on more than five years of street-based medical outreach, mobile crisis response, and harm reduction across Miami-Dade County, the University of Miami Miller School of Medicine, Jackson Health System / Jackson Memorial Hospital, and Dade County Street Response (DCSR) / Miami Street Medicine (MSM) have launched the Miami Street Medicine Training Initiative (MSMTI), supported by a five-year, $2.5 million grant (1 T35HP55565-01-00) from the Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA) Bureau of Health Workforce under the Primary Care Training and Enhancement: Residency Training in Street Medicine (PCTE-RTSM, HRSA-25-078) program.

Led by Principal Investigator and Project Director Armen Henderson, MD, MBA (Assistant Professor of Clinical Medicine and Founder/CEO of Dade County Street Response) and Co-Project Director Stefanie R. Brown, MD, MBA (Internal Medicine Residency Program Director and Section Chief of Med-Peds), in partnership with Med-Peds Program Director Jonathan Tolentino, MD, MS-HPE, Urban Health Track Director Kendra Van Kirk, MD, MAT, and Miami Street Medicine Founding Director and Curriculum Co-Developer Dan Bergholz, MD, the initiative embeds formal street medicine training across both the Internal Medicine (Urban Health Track) and Combined Internal Medicine-Pediatrics residency programs.

Addressing South Florida's Unsheltered Health Crisis

In Miami-Dade County, nearly 30% of households face severe housing instability, and unsheltered individuals confront extreme tropical heat, high rates of untreated hypertension (72% baseline prevalence in MSM's street cohort), and a 67% current / 86% lifetime prevalence of mental health and substance use disorders. Since 2020, Miami Street Medicine and Dade County Street Response have cared for more than 1,300 unique unsheltered patients across 4,000+ street encounters—demonstrating a statistically significant -9.25 mmHg reduction in systolic blood pressure (p < 0.01) and 45.3% full hypertension control delivered directly on the pavement.

A Seamless Hospital-to-Street Educational Ecosystem

Through two 4-week community clinical immersions, longitudinal continuity panels, and the 23-module "Street Classroom" curriculum, residents train across four integrated clinical environments:

  • Dade County Street Response & Miami Street Medicine: Weekly street rounds across Overtown, Downtown Miami, and countywide encampments, the Doctors Within Borders Free Urgent Care Clinic in Liberty City, and the 24/7 Freedom House Mobile Crisis Team.
  • Camillus Health Concern (FQHC) & Camillus House: Comprehensive Health Care for the Homeless primary care, Lazarus Project behavioral outreach, and post-hospitalization medical respite beds.
  • IDEA Syringe Services Program (IDEA Exchange): Florida's first legal syringe exchange, founded by Dr. Hansel Tookes, training residents in low-barrier sublingual and long-acting injectable buprenorphine, xylazine/fentanyl wound care, and Tele-Harm Reduction HIV/HCV care.
  • Jackson Memorial Hospital Inpatient Street Medicine Consult Service: "Street Medicine on the Floors" bedside consults preventing premature discharges and linking hospitalized unhoused patients to J-11 Jackson Card coverage and street follow-up.

Every field team pairs resident physicians with salaried Lived-Experience Peer Navigators and Spanish and Haitian Creole Language Concordance Observers, ensuring that the next generation of primary care physicians graduates equipped to practice radical hospitality and structural medicine wherever their patients live.
